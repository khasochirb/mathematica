## What this topic must teach

A student finishes this topic holding these ideas. Everything else in the
brief serves them.

1. **A piecewise function is several functions with a rule about which applies where.** Not one strange function. Once a student reads the condition first and the formula second, evaluation becomes routine.

2. **The absolute-value graph is a V because the negative half is reflected.** Understanding it as a reflection rather than a shape to memorise means transformations of it need no new rules.

3. **Open and closed circles say which piece owns the boundary.** They are the only thing distinguishing two otherwise identical graphs, and they carry real meaning.

**The error to design against:** Evaluating a piecewise function with the wrong branch — substituting into the formula next to the condition rather than the one whose condition the input satisfies.

If your Mongolian version lands those and a student can do the practice
set, the topic is right — however you got there.

Each lesson, and what the student must end up able to do:

### 1. `the-v-graph`

**Able to:** Graph y = |x| from its two-armed structure, identify the vertex, slopes, and symmetry.

**The idea that carries it:** y = |x| is two rays: slope −1 then +1, hinged at the vertex, mirror-symmetric across the y-axis.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Drawing the left arm with positive slope.
- Rounding the corner.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `pwg1-we1` — Make a table for $y = |x|$ at $x = -3, -1, 0, 1, 3$ and describe the shape.
- `pwg1-we2` — What are the slope of the left arm and right arm of $y = 3|x|$?
- `pwg1-we3` — Compare $y = |x|$ and $y = -|x|$ at $x = 4$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 2. `shifting-the-v`

**Able to:** Graph y = a|x − h| + k by reading the vertex (h, k), direction, and arm slopes from the equation.

**The idea that carries it:** Vertex $(h, k)$ reads straight off $a|x - h| + k$; arms leave it with slopes ±a.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Reading |x + 2| as a shift right.
- Swapping h and k.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `pwg2-we1` — Find the vertex and direction of $y = |x - 3| + 1$.
- `pwg2-we2` — Find the vertex of $y = |x + 2| - 4$ and its x-intercepts.
- `pwg2-we3` — Describe $y = -2|x - 1| + 6$: vertex, direction, arm slopes, maximum.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 3. `reading-piecewise-functions`

**Able to:** Evaluate piecewise-defined functions by selecting the correct piece for each input.

**The idea that carries it:** Find the piece whose interval owns the input; use only that formula. Boundaries belong to exactly one piece.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Using the formula that 'looks right' for the output.
- Letting a boundary belong to two pieces.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `pwg3-we1` — For $f(x) = \begin{cases} x + 5 & x < 0 \\ 2x & x \ge 0 \end{cases}$, find $f(-3)$, $f(0)$, and $f(4)$.
- `pwg3-we2` — Parking: free for $t \le 1$; $3(t - 1)$ dollars for $1 < t \le 4$; \$15 for $t > 4$. Find the cost at $t = 2.5$ and $t = 6$.
- `pwg3-we3` — Same garage: what does $t = 4$ cost — and $t = 4.01$?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 4. `graphing-piecewise`

**Able to:** Graph piecewise functions strip by strip, with correct open/closed endpoints, and spot jumps.

**The idea that carries it:** Paint each strip separately; filled vs open dots at every seam; matching values flow, mismatched values jump.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Extending a piece beyond its territory.
- Two filled dots at one seam.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `pwg4-we1` — Graph $f(x) = \begin{cases} x + 3 & x < 1 \\ 6 - 2x & x \ge 1 \end{cases}$ — do the pieces meet at $x = 1$?
- `pwg4-we2` — Graph $g(x) = \begin{cases} 2 & x < 0 \\ x - 1 & x \ge 0 \end{cases}$ — describe the seam at $x = 0$.
- `pwg4-we3` — Which x-values make $f(x) = \begin{cases} x + 3 & x < 1 \\ 6 - 2x & x \ge 1 \end{cases}$ equal to $0$?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 5. `step-functions`

**Able to:** Evaluate and graph step functions, and model tiered pricing with them.

**The idea that carries it:** Step functions are staircases: flat treads, jump risers, dot etiquette at every edge.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Interpolating between treads.
- Charging the lower tier at one gram past the edge.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `pwg5-we1` — Postage: $\le 20$g: \$1.50; $\le 50$g: \$2.40; $\le 100$g: \$3.60. Price letters of $18$ g, $50$ g, and $51$ g.
- `pwg5-we2` — A car park charges \$4 per hour OR PART: $4\lceil t \rceil$ style. What do $1.2$ h and $2$ h cost?
- `pwg5-we3` — Describe the graph of the floor function $y = \lfloor x \rfloor$ between $0$ and $3$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 6. `piecewise-in-action`

**Able to:** Build and use piecewise models for taxes, tiered rates, and wages, ensuring continuity where reality demands it.

**The idea that carries it:** Cumulative construction — previous total plus rate times excess — makes piecewise models continuous where fairness requires.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Applying the top rate to ALL income.
- Building a bracket from zero instead of the previous total.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `pwg6-we1` — Tax: $10\%$ up to \$10k; then $20\%$ up to \$40k. Build both pieces and find the tax on \$25{,}000.
- `pwg6-we2` — Verify that tax function is continuous at \$10{,}000.
- `pwg6-we3` — Wages: \$16/h up to $40$ h, then time-and-a-half. Build the overtime piece and price a $46$-hour week.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### Careful — English uses one word where Mongolian uses two

These are the ones that go wrong silently. The word above is the sense
this topic most likely means; check it against what you are actually
writing, and say so if the topic needs the other one.

**edge** — proposed **ирмэг**

> POLYSEMOUS, mildly. ирмэг is the edge of a solid (shipped and ЭШ alike) and, by extension, the edge of a histogram bin («ирмэг: $20$ нь $20$–$29$-ийг эхлүүлнэ») — that is the sense proposed here. Where "edge" means the margin of something written or laid out, shipped uses зах: "Line up the decimal points, not the right edges" → «Аравтын цэгүүдийг зэрэгцүүл, баруун захыг биш». So: ирмэг for geometry and bins, зах for the right-hand edge of a written column.

**absolute-value graph** — proposed **Модулт функцийн график**

> SENSE SPLIT, per Khas, 26 Aug 2026. These are not three renderings of one term — they are different things:
  • the VALUE  → «абсолют утга» or «үнэмлэхүй утга» (both correct)
  • the straight brackets | | → «модул» (the notation itself)
  • an absolute-value EQUATION → «модулт тэгшитгэл»
  • taking |x| of a number → «тооноос модул авах»
So the site's 'three ways' is not an inconsistency and needs no sweep. The earlier finding that ministry 12.1's «модул» should replace «абсолют утга» was WRONG: 12.1 is about modulus equations, which is the bracket sense.
  → this entry: the function under the brackets — bracket sense, so модул-.

## How it should sound

**«та», formal.** A student on this product is a customer, and «чи» is not
suitable for one. Formal is not the same as stiff — the English has jokes and
energy, and that should survive. Do not flatten it into textbook prose.

Quotes take «...». Proper names take their standard Mongolian forms. Product
names and symbols stay Latin.

## Writing maths

Only the rules that change what you type:

1. **Do not put Mongolian words inside a formula.** Write the formula in
   symbols and the words outside it. (If a word truly must sit inside one,
   mark it and Build will wrap it correctly.)
2. **Letters stay Latin** — `x`, `h`, point names, function names — even in
   Mongolian prose. Gloss one on first use if it helps.
3. **Bold is `**like this**`.** Single asterisks do not work and will appear
   on screen as asterisks.
4. **Units:** см, кг, мл read fine in a sentence.
