## What this topic must teach

A student finishes this topic holding these ideas. Everything else in the
brief serves them.

1. **Every method solves the same equation.** Square roots, factoring, completing the square and the formula are four routes to one place, and the point of the topic is knowing which route the shape of the equation asks for — not memorising four procedures. A student who leaves able to run all four but unable to choose has not learned the topic.

2. **±, not +.** `x² = 49` has two answers. This is the single most reliable error in the topic, and it is a reasoning failure, not a slip: squaring loses the sign, so un-squaring must put it back. Teach why the negative root is there and it stops being forgotten.

3. **The discriminant is a question you can ask before solving.** `b² − 4ac` tells you how many real solutions exist without finding them. Students who meet it as the last third of the formula never use it; students who meet it as a cheap look-ahead do.

**The error to design against:** Reading b and c without their signs, when the equation is not yet in standard form. Half the topic's wrong answers start here, before any method is chosen.

If your Mongolian version lands those and a student can do the practice
set, the topic is right — however you got there.

Each lesson, and what the student must end up able to do:

### 1. `meet-the-quadratics`

**Able to:** Recognize a quadratic equation, write it in standard form $ax^2+bx+c=0$, identify $a$, $b$, $c$, and check whether a number is a solution.

**The idea that carries it:** Quadratic equation: ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0). A solution makes it true; there are at most two.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Calling $x^2 = 25$ "already standard form".
- Reading $b$ and $c$ without their signs.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `qe1-we1` — Write $x^2 = 5x - 6$ in standard form and name $a$, $b$, $c$.
- `qe1-we2` — Is $x = 3$ a solution of $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$? Is $x = 1$?
- `qe1-we3` — A ball's height is $h = -5t^2 + 20t$. Verify it lands ($h=0$) at $t = 4$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 2. `solving-by-square-roots`

**Able to:** Solve quadratics of the form $x^2 = k$ and $(x+a)^2 = k$ by taking square roots, remembering both the positive and negative root.

**The idea that carries it:** x² = k → x = ±√k (two solutions for k > 0, one for k = 0, none real for k < 0).

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Writing only $x = \sqrt{k}$ and forgetting the negative root.
- Un-squaring before the square is alone.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `qe2-we1` — Solve $x^2 = 49$.
- `qe2-we2` — Solve $x^2 = 20$. Give exact answers.
- `qe2-we3` — Solve $(x-3)^2 = 16$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 3. `solving-by-factoring`

**Able to:** Solve quadratic equations by moving everything to one side, factoring, and applying the zero product property.

**The idea that carries it:** Get 0 on one side, factor, set each factor to 0. A product is zero only when a factor is.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Using the zero product property when the right side isn't zero.
- Dividing both sides by $x$.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `qe3-we1` — Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.
- `qe3-we2` — Solve $x^2 + 3x = 40$.
- `qe3-we3` — Solve $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 4. `completing-the-square`

**Able to:** Turn $x^2 + bx$ into a perfect square by adding $(b/2)^2$, and use it to solve quadratics that don't factor nicely.

**The idea that carries it:** Add (b/2)² to complete x² + bx into (x + b/2)². Balance the equation, then solve by square roots.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Adding $b^2$ instead of $(b/2)^2$.
- Patching the left side and forgetting the right.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `qe4-we1` — What must be added to $x^2 + 10x$ to complete the square?
- `qe4-we2` — Solve $x^2 + 6x + 5 = 0$ by completing the square.
- `qe4-we3` — Solve $x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$ exactly.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 5. `the-quadratic-formula`

**Able to:** Solve any quadratic with $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$, keeping signs straight and answers exact.

**The idea that carries it:** x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / (2a) — works on every quadratic, no exceptions.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Feeding in $b$ without its sign.
- Dividing only the root part by $2a$.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `qe5-we1` — Solve $x^2 - 2x - 8 = 0$ with the quadratic formula.
- `qe5-we2` — Solve $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$.
- `qe5-we3` — Solve $x^2 + 3x + 1 = 0$ exactly.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 6. `the-discriminant`

**Able to:** Use the discriminant $b^2-4ac$ to count real solutions without solving, and pick the fastest solving method for a given quadratic.

**The idea that carries it:** D = b² − 4ac: positive → 2 real solutions, zero → 1, negative → 0. Check the forecast, then pick your route.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Computing $b^2 - 4ac$ with sign slips in $-4ac$.
- Saying $D = 0$ means "no solutions".

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `qe6-we1` — How many real solutions does $x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0$ have?
- `qe6-we2` — How many real solutions does $x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0$ have?
- `qe6-we3` — Count the real solutions of $2x^2 + 3x - 2 = 0$, then find them.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

## What you owe for every example you change

A worked example or try-it problem with different numbers needs a new
**check** — a line of maths that says what must be true, so we can verify it
mechanically without a second person re-solving it.

**You do not write these.** Write the example and its answer in plain
Mongolian, and Build turns it into the assertion. If an example's answer
cannot be stated exactly, the example is not ready.

**Practice and test-yourself problems are different: do not change their
numbers.** Those are graded against a stored answer key and every student's
history is keyed to them. Rewrite the *wording* freely; leave the maths.

### Careful — English uses one word where Mongolian uses two

These are the ones that go wrong silently. The word above is the sense
this topic most likely means; check it against what you are actually
writing, and say so if the topic needs the other one.

**factor** — proposed **хуваагч**

> One English key, two live Mongolian terms: a number's factor/divisor is «хуваагч» (164× in production, not in the ministry standard), while an algebraic factor is «үржигдэхүүн» (ministry 10.2 «үржигдэхүүн болгон задлах»; 80× in production). Needs two keys — and note «хуваагдагч» (105×) is the multiple/dividend, easy to confuse.

**count** — proposed **тоолох**

> POLYSEMOUS. Verb "to count" = тоолох (ministry 12.14, and shipped throughout). Noun "the count" = тоо when it means how many items («Mean = sum ÷ count» → «Дундаж = нийлбэр ÷ тоо»), but тооллого when it means the tally/inventory itself («the small raised exponent is the count» → «илтгэгч нь тооллого»; "take inventory" → «Тооллого хий»). This entry proposes the verb. Do not write тоолол.

## How it should sound

**«та», formal.** A student on this product is a customer, and «чи» is not
suitable for one. Formal is not the same as stiff — the English has jokes and
energy, and that should survive. Do not flatten it into textbook prose.

Quotes take «...». Proper names take their standard Mongolian forms. Product
names and symbols stay Latin.

> Note if you have seen earlier material: grades 6 and 7 are written in «чи»,
> which is now the wrong register. Do not copy their tone.

## Writing maths

Only the rules that change what you type:

1. **Do not put Mongolian words inside a formula.** Write the formula in
   symbols and the words outside it. (If a word truly must sit inside one,
   mark it and Build will wrap it correctly.)
2. **Letters stay Latin** — `x`, `h`, point names, function names — even in
   Mongolian prose. Gloss one on first use if it helps.
3. **Bold is `**like this**`.** Single asterisks do not work and will appear
   on screen as asterisks.
4. **Units:** см, кг, мл read fine in a sentence.
