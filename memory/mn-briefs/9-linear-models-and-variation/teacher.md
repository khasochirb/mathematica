## What this topic must teach

A student finishes this topic holding these ideas. Everything else in the
brief serves them.

1. **Slope is a rate with units.** Not a number on a graph — metres per second, tögrög per kilo. A student who reads slope with its units can interpret any model; one who does not is doing arithmetic without meaning.

2. **Direct variation is a line through the origin.** y = kx is the special case where nothing exists when x is zero. Seeing it as a restricted line rather than a separate topic halves what has to be learned.

3. **The intercept is the starting amount.** In a real model it is what you have before anything happens, and it is where most interpretation questions live.

**The error to design against:** Computing slope as run over rise. The order is not arbitrary — it is what makes slope a rate of change of y.

If your Mongolian version lands those and a student can do the practice
set, the topic is right — however you got there.

Each lesson, and what the student must end up able to do:

### 1. `building-linear-models`

**Able to:** Build y = mx + b models from stories, interpreting slope as rate and intercept as starting value.

**The idea that carries it:** Starting value → b, rate (with sign) → m; then y = mx + b answers every question in the story.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Swapping the roles of m and b.
- Dropping the negative on depletion rates.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `lmv1-we1` — A candle is $25$ cm tall and burns $2$ cm/h. Build the model and find the height after $3$ hours.
- `lmv1-we2` — Same candle: when does it burn out?
- `lmv1-we3` — A phone battery is at $80\%$ and drains $12\%$ per hour of video. Model, and find the charge after $2.5$ hours.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 2. `models-from-data`

**Able to:** Find the rate from two data points, recover the starting value, and write the linear model.

**The idea that carries it:** $m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ from the two points, then one substitution recovers b — and the other point audits.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Dividing Δx by Δy.
- Subtracting the points in mismatched order.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `lmv2-we1` — Shoots cost \$95 for $1$ h and \$215 for $4$ h. Find the model.
- `lmv2-we2` — A tank holds $46$ L after $2$ min of filling and $94$ L after $8$ min. Rate, starting amount, and model?
- `lmv2-we3` — A spring is $14$ cm with $20$ g hanging and $20$ cm with $50$ g. Predict its natural (unloaded) length.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 3. `direct-variation`

**Able to:** Model direct variation with y = kx, find k from one pair, and recognize direct variation in tables and graphs.

**The idea that carries it:** Direct variation is y = kx: line through the origin, constant ratio y/x, perfect doubling.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Calling every linear relationship 'direct variation'.
- Finding k by subtracting instead of dividing.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `lmv3-we1` — $y$ varies directly with $x$, and $y = 24$ when $x = 4$. Find $k$ and the value of $y$ at $x = 7$.
- `lmv3-we2` — A car uses fuel directly with distance: $18$ L for $240$ km. How much for $400$ km?
- `lmv3-we3` — Which table shows direct variation: A: $(2, 5), (4, 10), (6, 15)$ or B: $(2, 5), (4, 9), (6, 13)$?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 4. `inverse-variation`

**Able to:** Model inverse variation with y = k/x (xy = k), find k, and distinguish inverse from direct variation.

**The idea that carries it:** Inverse variation locks the product: xy = k. Double one factor, halve the other.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Finding k by dividing in an inverse problem.
- Thinking 'inverse' means y is negative or decreasing linearly.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `lmv4-we1` — A $240$ km trip: $y$ hours at $x$ km/h vary inversely. Find the time at $96$ km/h.
- `lmv4-we2` — $y$ varies inversely with $x$; $y = 6$ when $x = 4$. Find $y$ when $x = 8$.
- `lmv4-we3` — Which varies inversely: A: $(2, 18), (3, 12), (6, 6)$ or B: $(2, 4), (3, 6), (6, 12)$?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 5. `classifying-relationships`

**Able to:** Classify tables and stories as direct variation, inverse variation, linear-with-intercept, or neither.

**The idea that carries it:** Compute y/x and xy for every row: constant ratio → direct; constant product → inverse; constant jumps → linear; else neither.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Stopping after the ratio test.
- Classifying by whether y goes up or down.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `lmv5-we1` — Classify: $(1, 4), (2, 8), (5, 20)$.
- `lmv5-we2` — Classify: $(1, 24), (2, 12), (4, 6)$.
- `lmv5-we3` — Classify: $(1, 7), (2, 9), (3, 11)$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 6. `variation-in-action`

**Able to:** Solve applied problems by selecting and combining direct variation, inverse variation, and linear models.

**The idea that carries it:** Choose the model from the story's structure, solve with its one constant, and sanity-check the direction.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Balancing a lever with addition.
- Using yesterday's k in today's problem.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `lmv6-we1` — A $60$ kg parent sits $1$ m from a seesaw's pivot. Where must a $20$ kg child sit to balance?
- `lmv6-we2` — A grant of \$5{,}400 is split equally. Compare shares for $12$ vs $18$ families.
- `lmv6-we3` — Translation costs vary directly at \$0.14 per word; rush jobs add a flat \$25. Cost of a $2{,}300$-word rush job?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

## What you owe for every example you change

A worked example or try-it problem with different numbers needs a new
**check** — a line of maths that says what must be true, so we can verify it
mechanically without a second person re-solving it.

**You do not write these.** Write the example and its answer in plain
Mongolian, and Build turns it into the assertion. If an example's answer
cannot be stated exactly, the example is not ready.

**Practice and test-yourself problems are different: do not change their
numbers.** Those are graded against a stored answer key and every student's
history is keyed to them. Rewrite the *wording* freely; leave the maths.

this topic most likely means; check it against what you are actually
writing, and say so if the topic needs the other one.

## How it should sound

**«та», formal.** A student on this product is a customer, and «чи» is not
suitable for one. Formal is not the same as stiff — the English has jokes and
energy, and that should survive. Do not flatten it into textbook prose.

Quotes take «...». Proper names take their standard Mongolian forms. Product
names and symbols stay Latin.

> Note if you have seen earlier material: grades 6 and 7 are written in «чи»,
> which is now the wrong register. Do not copy their tone.

## Writing maths

Only the rules that change what you type:

1. **Do not put Mongolian words inside a formula.** Write the formula in
   symbols and the words outside it. (If a word truly must sit inside one,
   mark it and Build will wrap it correctly.)
2. **Letters stay Latin** — `x`, `h`, point names, function names — even in
   Mongolian prose. Gloss one on first use if it helps.
3. **Bold is `**like this**`.** Single asterisks do not work and will appear
   on screen as asterisks.
4. **Units:** см, кг, мл read fine in a sentence.
