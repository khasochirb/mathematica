## What this topic must teach

A student finishes this topic holding these ideas. Everything else in the
brief serves them.

1. **One input, one output. That is the whole definition.** Everything else in the topic — the vertical line test, function notation, domain and range — is a consequence of it. Teach the rule before any of its tools.

2. **f(x) is a name, not a multiplication.** The single most damaging misreading in early algebra: students see f(x) and multiply. Say it out loud, in Mongolian, the first time it appears.

3. **Domain and range are questions about what is allowed and what comes out.** Not formulas to memorise. Ask them of every function the lesson meets.

**The error to design against:** Reading f(x) as f times x, and then f(2) as 2f. Worth catching in the first lesson, because everything after it inherits the error.

If your Mongolian version lands those and a student can do the practice
set, the topic is right — however you got there.

Each lesson, and what the student must end up able to do:

### 1. `what-is-a-function`

**Able to:** Define functions as one-output-per-input rules, and test relations (sets, tables, mappings) for function-hood.

**The idea that carries it:** Function = every input has exactly one output. Repeated inputs must agree; repeated outputs are harmless.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Rejecting a relation because outputs repeat.
- Judging by the formula's looks instead of the pairing.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `fun1-we1` — Is $\{(2, 5), (3, 7), (4, 5)\}$ a function?
- `fun1-we2` — Is $\{(1, 4), (2, 6), (1, 8)\}$ a function?
- `fun1-we3` — A table maps each city to its country. Is it a function? What about each country to its cities?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 2. `function-notation`

**Able to:** Read and evaluate function notation, including expressions as inputs, and solve f(x) = k for x.

**The idea that carries it:** $f(3)$ = 'feed 3 to machine f'; substitute the whole input everywhere x appears; f(x) = k runs the machine backwards.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Treating f(3) as f times 3.
- Substituting an expression input into only the first x.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `fun2-we1` — Given $f(x) = 2x + 1$, find $f(3)$, $f(0)$, and $f(-4)$.
- `fun2-we2` — Given $g(x) = x^2 - 3x$, find $g(5)$.
- `fun2-we3` — Given $f(x) = 2x + 1$, solve $f(x) = 15$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 3. `domain-and-range`

**Able to:** Find domains and ranges from ordered pairs, rules, and real contexts, spotting excluded inputs.

**The idea that carries it:** Domain = legal inputs (watch for division by zero and context); range = the outputs actually produced.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Reporting the range as 'everything the formula could make'.
- Forgetting context restrictions.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `fun3-we1` — Find the domain and range of $\{(1, 3), (2, 5), (4, 3)\}$.
- `fun3-we2` — Find the domain of $f(x) = \frac{6}{x - 4}$.
- `fun3-we3` — A theater seats $250$; tickets cost \$12. Revenue is $R(t) = 12t$. State the realistic domain and range.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 4. `graphs-and-the-vertical-line-test`

**Able to:** Read function values from graphs and apply the vertical line test to identify functions.

**The idea that carries it:** Graph reading: over to x, up to the curve, out to y. Function ⟺ every vertical line hits at most once.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Applying a HORIZONTAL line test to check function-hood.
- Reading f(3) by finding 3 on the y-axis.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `fun4-we1` — A graph passes through $(2, 5)$ and $(4, 5)$ but has no other points at $x = 2$ or $x = 4$. What are $f(2)$ and $f(4)$? Does the repetition break anyt
- `fun4-we2` — A circle of radius $3$ centered at the origin: function or not?
- `fun4-we3` — The graph of $f$ crosses the horizontal line $y = 4$ at $x = -1$ and $x = 6$. What does that tell you?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 5. `linear-vs-nonlinear`

**Able to:** Classify functions as linear or nonlinear from tables (constant first differences), equations, and graphs.

**The idea that carries it:** Linear = constant first differences = straight line = y = mx + b. One failed check fails them all.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Calling any increasing table linear.
- Forgetting to check that x steps evenly first.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `fun5-we1` — Table: $x = 1, 2, 3, 4$; $y = 5, 8, 11, 14$. Linear?
- `fun5-we2` — Table: $x = 1, 2, 3, 4$; $y = 1, 4, 9, 16$. Linear?
- `fun5-we3` — Classify: $y = 4 - \frac{x}{2}$, $\;y = x(x + 1)$, $\;y = \frac{6}{x}$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 6. `interpreting-graphs`

**Able to:** Describe where functions increase, decrease, and stay constant; identify intercepts and extremes; match graphs to stories.

**The idea that carries it:** Read graphs left to right as stories: rises, falls, flats, anchored by intercepts and peaks.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Describing increase/decrease with y-values.
- Reading a flat distance-time segment as constant speed.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `fun6-we1` — A hiker's distance-time graph rises for $t = 0$ to $2$ h, is flat from $2$ to $3$, then rises less steeply from $3$ to $5$. Tell the story.
- `fun6-we2` — A ball's height is $h(t) = 20t - 5t^2$ (meters). Compute $h(0), h(2), h(4)$ and locate the peak time.
- `fun6-we3` — A bank balance graph starts at $y = 120$, falls to cross zero at $t = 8$, and keeps falling. Interpret both intercepts.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

## What you owe for every example you change

A worked example or try-it problem with different numbers needs a new
**check** — a line of maths that says what must be true, so we can verify it
mechanically without a second person re-solving it.

**You do not write these.** Write the example and its answer in plain
Mongolian, and Build turns it into the assertion. If an example's answer
cannot be stated exactly, the example is not ready.

**Practice and test-yourself problems are different: do not change their
numbers.** Those are graded against a stored answer key and every student's
history is keyed to them. Rewrite the *wording* freely; leave the maths.

### Careful — English uses one word where Mongolian uses two

These are the ones that go wrong silently. The word above is the sense
this topic most likely means; check it against what you are actually
writing, and say so if the topic needs the other one.

**range** — proposed **далайц**

> One English key, two unrelated Mongolian terms: the statistical range is «далайц» (10.13, 11.11) but a function's range is «утгын муж; дүр» (glossary; ministry 11.3 «тодорхойлогдох муж ба дүр»). Split into two keys before the calculus hubs land.

**increase** — proposed **өсөх**

> POLYSEMOUS by form. The intransitive "increase / be increasing" is өсөх — ministry, glossary and shipped all agree, and its opposite is буурах. But the noun "an increase" is өсөлт, which is what the percent unit actually ships: «Хувиар өсөлт ба бууралт» = Percent Increase & Decrease, «$25\%$-ийн өсөлт» = a 25% increase. And the transitive "increase something" is ихэсгэх/өсгөх («гурав дахин ихэсгэвэл»). So: өсөх for a quantity rising or a function increasing, өсөлт for the measured increase, ихэсгэх when a person increases a value. Do not use нэмэгдэх (which shipped uses for parts adding up across the origin) as a synonym for the function property.

## How it should sound

**«та», formal.** A student on this product is a customer, and «чи» is not
suitable for one. Formal is not the same as stiff — the English has jokes and
energy, and that should survive. Do not flatten it into textbook prose.

Quotes take «...». Proper names take their standard Mongolian forms. Product
names and symbols stay Latin.

> Note if you have seen earlier material: grades 6 and 7 are written in «чи»,
> which is now the wrong register. Do not copy their tone.

## Writing maths

Only the rules that change what you type:

1. **Do not put Mongolian words inside a formula.** Write the formula in
   symbols and the words outside it. (If a word truly must sit inside one,
   mark it and Build will wrap it correctly.)
2. **Letters stay Latin** — `x`, `h`, point names, function names — even in
   Mongolian prose. Gloss one on first use if it helps.
3. **Bold is `**like this**`.** Single asterisks do not work and will appear
   on screen as asterisks.
4. **Units:** см, кг, мл read fine in a sentence.
