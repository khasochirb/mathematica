Each lesson, and what the student must end up able to do:

### 1. `multi-step-inequalities`

**Able to:** Solve inequalities requiring distribution and variables on both sides, and express solutions on the number line.

**The idea that carries it:** Equation pipeline + the flip rule = every linear inequality; verify with a test point.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Flipping when subtracting a variable term.
- Shading toward the variable's side of the symbol.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `iav1-we1` — Solve $25h + 40 > 190$.
- `iav1-we2` — Solve $3(x - 2) \le 5x + 8$.
- `iav1-we3` — Solve $7 - 2x > 15$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 2. `compound-inequalities`

**Able to:** Solve AND (between) and OR (either side) compound inequalities and graph their solution sets.

**The idea that carries it:** AND = overlap (a segment); OR = union (two rays). Sandwiches solve three parts at once.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Operating on only two of a sandwich's three parts.
- Writing an OR answer as a sandwich.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `iav2-we1` — Solve $-5 \le 2x + 1 < 7$.
- `iav2-we2` — Solve: $3x + 2 < -4$ or $2x - 1 > 5$.
- `iav2-we3` — A coaster requires height $h$ with $120 \le h \le 195$ (cm). Is a $118$ cm child admitted? A $195$ cm adult?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 3. `absolute-value-equations`

**Able to:** Solve absolute-value equations by splitting into two cases, checking for extraneous cases and no-solution traps.

**The idea that carries it:** Isolate the bars, check the right side's sign, then split: $X = k$ or $X = -k$.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Splitting before isolating.
- Negating the whole equation instead of the right side.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `iav3-we1` — Solve $|x - 500| = 4$.
- `iav3-we2` — Solve $2|x - 1| + 3 = 11$.
- `iav3-we3` — Solve $|3x + 2| = -7$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 4. `absolute-value-inequalities`

**Able to:** Solve |X| < k (between) and |X| > k (outside) inequalities and connect them to compound inequalities.

**The idea that carries it:** Less-than → between (AND); greater-than → outside (OR). Isolate first; centers and radii do the rest.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Unpacking |X| > k as a sandwich.
- Forgetting to flip the second tail's inequality.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `iav4-we1` — Solve $|v - 500| \le 4$.
- `iav4-we2` — Solve $|2x - 3| > 5$.
- `iav4-we3` — Solve $3|x + 1| - 2 < 7$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 5. `inequality-word-problems`

**Able to:** Translate constraints into plain, compound, or absolute-value inequalities and interpret solution sets in context.

**The idea that carries it:** Match the phrase to the tool — plain, sandwich, or bars — then solve with yesterday's machinery.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Turning 'within' into a plain one-sided inequality.
- Averaging only the known scores.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `iav5-we1` — Riya scored $82, 88, 79$ on three tests. What must she score on the fourth for an average of at least $85$?
- `iav5-we2` — A drone carries at most $2.5$ kg and already holds a $0.7$ kg camera. How many $0.3$ kg sensor packs can it add?
- `iav5-we3` — A part passes inspection when its length is within $0.3$ mm of $120$ mm. Write and solve the inequality.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 6. `inequalities-in-action`

**Able to:** Combine all inequality types to analyze profit, constraints, and tolerance problems.

**The idea that carries it:** Break-even = the fence; profit = the side you want. Stack constraints with AND; quality is a shrinking band.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Calling the break-even count 'profitable'.
- Checking constraints one at a time and keeping partial answers.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `iav6-we1` — Cases sell at \$15, cost \$6 to make, fixed costs \$1{,}800/month. How many sales make a profitable month?
- `iav6-we2` — A budget requires: venue \$400, food \$12 per guest, total at most \$1{,}000, and at least $30$ guests. Feasible guest counts?
- `iav6-we3` — A factory targets a $5\%$ defect rate, acceptable within $2$ percentage points. Express the acceptable band.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

## What you owe for every example you change

A worked example or try-it problem with different numbers needs a new
**check** — a line of maths that says what must be true, so we can verify it
mechanically without a second person re-solving it.

**You do not write these.** Write the example and its answer in plain
Mongolian, and Build turns it into the assertion. If an example's answer
cannot be stated exactly, the example is not ready.

**Practice and test-yourself problems are different: do not change their
numbers.** Those are graded against a stored answer key and every student's
history is keyed to them. Rewrite the *wording* freely; leave the maths.

### Careful — English uses one word where Mongolian uses two

These are the ones that go wrong silently. The word above is the sense
this topic most likely means; check it against what you are actually
writing, and say so if the topic needs the other one.

**absolute value** — proposed **абсолют утга**

> SENSE SPLIT, per Khas, 26 Aug 2026. These are not three renderings of one term — they are different things:
  • the VALUE  → «абсолют утга» or «үнэмлэхүй утга» (both correct)
  • the straight brackets | | → «модул» (the notation itself)
  • an absolute-value EQUATION → «модулт тэгшитгэл»
  • taking |x| of a number → «тооноос модул авах»
So the site's 'three ways' is not an inconsistency and needs no sweep. The earlier finding that ministry 12.1's «модул» should replace «абсолют утга» was WRONG: 12.1 is about modulus equations, which is the bracket sense.
  → this entry: the value. «үнэмлэхүй утга» is equally correct.

**translate** — proposed **хөрвүүлэх**

> Words→symbols sense only; one shipped string instead says «болго». The geometry verb is «параллель зөөх» (MoE 10.11) and the two must not merge — same English word, two Mongolian verbs.

## How it should sound

**«та», formal.** A student on this product is a customer, and «чи» is not
suitable for one. Formal is not the same as stiff — the English has jokes and
energy, and that should survive. Do not flatten it into textbook prose.

Quotes take «...». Proper names take their standard Mongolian forms. Product
names and symbols stay Latin.

> Note if you have seen earlier material: grades 6 and 7 are written in «чи»,
> which is now the wrong register. Do not copy their tone.

## Writing maths

Only the rules that change what you type:

1. **Do not put Mongolian words inside a formula.** Write the formula in
   symbols and the words outside it. (If a word truly must sit inside one,
   mark it and Build will wrap it correctly.)
2. **Letters stay Latin** — `x`, `h`, point names, function names — even in
   Mongolian prose. Gloss one on first use if it helps.
3. **Bold is `**like this**`.** Single asterisks do not work and will appear
   on screen as asterisks.
4. **Units:** см, кг, мл read fine in a sentence.
