## What this topic must teach

A student finishes this topic holding these ideas. Everything else in the
brief serves them.

1. **These are fractions, with all the same rules.** Common denominators, cancelling common factors, multiply straight across, divide by flipping. A student who sees them as fractions already knows the operations; one who sees them as a new topic learns four procedures again.

2. **You may only cancel factors, never terms.** The single most common error in the topic, and it comes from cancelling what looks the same rather than what is multiplied. Worth teaching as a rule with a reason attached.

3. **Excluded values are part of the answer.** A denominator that could be zero restricts the domain, and the restriction survives simplification — even after the offending factor cancels. Dropping it changes what the expression means.

**The error to design against:** Cancelling across a sum: in (x+3)/(x+5) the x's cannot go. Nearly universal, and it needs numbers to disprove, not a rule.

If your Mongolian version lands those and a student can do the practice
set, the topic is right — however you got there.

Each lesson, and what the student must end up able to do:

### 1. `meet-rational-expressions`

**Able to:** Evaluate rational expressions and find their excluded values — the inputs that make the denominator zero.

**The idea that carries it:** Rational expression = polynomial ÷ polynomial. Excluded values are where the DENOMINATOR is zero.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Excluding values that zero the NUMERATOR.
- Stopping at one excluded value when the denominator is quadratic.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `re1-we1` — Evaluate $\frac{x+1}{x-3}$ at $x = 5$ and at $x = -1$.
- `re1-we2` — Find the excluded value(s) of $\frac{x+1}{x-3}$.
- `re1-we3` — Find all excluded values of $\frac{5}{x^2 - x - 6}$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 2. `simplifying-rational-expressions`

**Able to:** Simplify rational expressions by factoring numerator and denominator and canceling common FACTORS — never terms.

**The idea that carries it:** Factor top and bottom; cancel whole FACTORS only. Terms in a sum never cancel.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Canceling terms: $\tfrac{x+3}{3} \to x$ or $\tfrac{x^2+5}{x} \to x + 5$.
- Forgetting the original excluded values after simplifying.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `re2-we1` — Simplify $\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+5x+6}$.
- `re2-we2` — Simplify $\frac{3x+12}{6x}$.
- `re2-we3` — Simplify $\frac{x^2-4x}{x^2-16}$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 3. `multiplying-and-dividing`

**Able to:** Multiply rational expressions by factoring and cross-canceling; divide by flipping the second fraction and multiplying.

**The idea that carries it:** Factor → cross-cancel → multiply across. Divide = keep, change, flip, then multiply.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Flipping the FIRST fraction when dividing.
- Multiplying straight across without factoring, then facing a monster.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `re3-we1` — Multiply $\frac{3x}{x+2} \cdot \frac{x+2}{6}$.
- `re3-we2` — Multiply $\frac{x^2-9}{4x} \cdot \frac{8x^2}{x+3}$.
- `re3-we3` — Divide $\frac{x^2-4}{x} \div \frac{x+2}{x^2}$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 4. `adding-and-subtracting`

**Able to:** Add and subtract rational expressions by building the least common denominator, and distribute subtraction across the whole numerator.

**The idea that carries it:** Same denominator: combine tops. Different: build the LCD, rebuild each fraction, then combine — minus distributes over the whole top.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Adding tops AND bottoms: $\tfrac{1}{x} + \tfrac{1}{x} = \tfrac{2}{2x}$.
- Dropping the parentheses when subtracting: $\tfrac{5x - x + 6}{x-2}$.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `re4-we1` — Add $\frac{3}{x} + \frac{2}{x^2}$.
- `re4-we2` — Add $\frac{2}{x+1} + \frac{3}{x-1}$.
- `re4-we3` — Subtract $\frac{5x}{x-2} - \frac{x+6}{x-2}$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 5. `solving-rational-equations`

**Able to:** Solve rational equations by clearing denominators with the LCD, and reject extraneous solutions that hit excluded values.

**The idea that carries it:** Multiply both sides by the LCD to clear all fractions; solve; then reject any answer that's an excluded value.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Multiplying only the fraction terms by the LCD and skipping the plain numbers.
- Reporting an extraneous solution as the answer.

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `re5-we1` — Solve $\frac{3}{x} + \frac{1}{2} = 2$.
- `re5-we2` — Solve $\frac{5}{x+1} = \frac{3}{x-1}$.
- `re5-we3` — Solve $\frac{x}{x-3} = \frac{3}{x-3} + 2$.

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

### 6. `work-and-rate-problems`

**Able to:** Solve combined-work and rate problems by adding rates as rational expressions and clearing the resulting equation.

**The idea that carries it:** Rate = 1/time. Rates add: 1/a + 1/b = 1/t. Clear the LCD and solve — then sanity-check the time.

**Where students break** — the English warns about these, and they are
worth keeping however you phrase them:
- Adding the TIMES: "6 h + 3 h = 9 h together".
- Averaging the times: "(6+3)/2 = 4.5 h together".

**3 worked examples.** Yours to choose — different numbers, a
different context, a Mongolian setting instead of an American one. What
each must *do* is fixed:
- `re6-we1` — A painter takes 6 h, a helper 3 h. How long together?
- `re6-we2` — Pipe A fills a tank in 4 h; pipe B in 12 h. Both open: how long?
- `re6-we3` — Two printers finish a stack together in 4 h. Alone, printer A takes 6 h. How long for B alone?

**2 try-it problems**, same freedom and same condition.

## What you owe for every example you change

A worked example or try-it problem with different numbers needs a new
**check** — a line of maths that says what must be true, so we can verify it
mechanically without a second person re-solving it.

**You do not write these.** Write the example and its answer in plain
Mongolian, and Build turns it into the assertion. If an example's answer
cannot be stated exactly, the example is not ready.

**Practice and test-yourself problems are different: do not change their
numbers.** Those are graded against a stored answer key and every student's
history is keyed to them. Rewrite the *wording* freely; leave the maths.

## How it should sound

**«та», formal.** A student on this product is a customer, and «чи» is not
suitable for one. Formal is not the same as stiff — the English has jokes and
energy, and that should survive. Do not flatten it into textbook prose.

Quotes take «...». Proper names take their standard Mongolian forms. Product
names and symbols stay Latin.

> Note if you have seen earlier material: grades 6 and 7 are written in «чи»,
> which is now the wrong register. Do not copy their tone.

## Writing maths

Only the rules that change what you type:

1. **Do not put Mongolian words inside a formula.** Write the formula in
   symbols and the words outside it. (If a word truly must sit inside one,
   mark it and Build will wrap it correctly.)
2. **Letters stay Latin** — `x`, `h`, point names, function names — even in
   Mongolian prose. Gloss one on first use if it helps.
3. **Bold is `**like this**`.** Single asterisks do not work and will appear
   on screen as asterisks.
4. **Units:** см, кг, мл read fine in a sentence.
